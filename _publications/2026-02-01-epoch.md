abstract: >-
  LLM-based automated heuristic design (AHD) discovers executable heuristics for NP-hard combinatorial optimization problems (COPs), where a large language model (LLM) generates candidate heuristics inside an evolutionary search. In existing frameworks, the generator either stays frozen or is fine-tuned on a scalar fitness signal that cannot show which instances each heuristic covers. Reflection-based frameworks improve heuristics with natural-language feedback, but the feedback stays in the prompt and the generator itself does not learn from it. We propose EPOCH, which sets instance coverage as the objective of the evolutionary search and fine-tunes the generator to internalize reflection. Parent selection, operator allocation, and stagnation recovery all optimize how well the heuristics jointly cover the training instances, so the search returns a complementary ensemble of heuristics. The generator learns from its own search through experiential preference optimization. A teacher LLM returns a natural-language insight on the generated heuristics, and the generator produces new heuristics with this insight. The resulting improvements join the preference pairs used for direct preference optimization (DPO). Because DPO only teaches the generator to rank these improvements, a reflective self-distillation (RSD) term makes it more likely to generate them on its own, and the gains accumulate over the long horizon of the evolutionary search. In experiments across COPs and open-source LLM backbones, the heuristic ensembles discovered by EPOCH outperform inference-only and fine-tuned LLM-based AHD baselines and generalize across problem scales.
